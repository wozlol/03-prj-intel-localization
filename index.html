Sustainability Through the Ages

Our commitment to innovation has shaped a more sustainable future for technology and our planet.

Explore the Timeline

Milestones

The company begins as a semiconductor and memory technology pioneer, building the technologies that power future computing.
1968 Foundation

Intel Founded

Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore rename the newly formed company NM Electronics to Intel Corporation, laying the foundation for decades of technological innovation.

The 4004 introduces the microprocessor era and begins Intel's long role in expanding computing access.
1971 Innovation

First Microprocessor

Intel debuts the 4004, the world's first commercial microprocessor, igniting the microprocessor revolution and propelling the future of computing devices.

The x86 architecture becomes a foundation for flexible and widely deployed computing platforms.
1978 Architecture

8086 Processor

Launch of the 8086 processor, establishing the x86 architecture that drives countless PCs and servers in the modern era.

The 386 expands the capabilities of personal computers and sets a new benchmark for mainstream computing.
1985 Performance

386 Processor

Intel introduces the 386 processor with 32-bit architecture, ushering in a new era of performance and multitasking for personal computers.

Intel reports the highest point in operational greenhouse gas emissions before long-term environmental reduction programs accelerate.
2006 Emissions

Peak GHG Emissions

This year marks Intel's highest annual greenhouse gas emissions for operations. Over subsequent years, Intel invests heavily in chemical abatement, renewable energy, and energy-efficient manufacturing to reverse this trend.

RISE connects Intel's responsible product strategy to public goals across sustainability, inclusion, and enabling technology.
2020 Strategy

RISE Strategy

Intel launches its RISE (Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable, Enabling) strategy and 2030 goals, aiming to drive industry-wide progress on climate action, water stewardship, and waste reduction.

The 2040 net-zero target formalizes long-term operational climate reduction commitments.
2022 Climate

Net-Zero By 2040

Intel announces its commitment to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1 and 2) across its global operations by 2040, building on years of environmental initiatives.

Intel reports high renewable electricity coverage as part of a broader strategy to lower manufacturing emissions.
2023 Energy

Renewable Electricity

The company achieves 99% renewable electricity usage worldwide, helping to drastically lower carbon emissions and driving progress toward Intel's long-term sustainability goals.

The summit advances cross-sector cooperation around supply chains, climate, water, and responsible manufacturing.
2024 Collaboration

Sustainability Summit

Intel hosts its first Sustainability Summit, bringing suppliers, government officials, and industry leaders together to collaborate on next-generation sustainable semiconductor manufacturing.

Sustainability in Focus

Strategy, Manufacturing, and Climate

Strategy

In 2020, Intel introduced its RISE strategy, a set of 2030 goals covering climate action, water stewardship, and waste reduction. Two years later, Intel extended that commitment further, targeting net-zero greenhouse gas emissions across its own operations by 2040.

View on Timeline

Manufacturing

After operational emissions peaked in 2006, Intel invested in chemical abatement and energy-efficient manufacturing to bring that number down. In 2024, Intel hosted its first Sustainability Summit, bringing suppliers and industry leaders together to work on next-generation sustainable semiconductor manufacturing.

View on Timeline

Climate

Intel's operational emissions peaked in 2006. Since then, the company has moved toward renewable electricity, reporting 99% renewable electricity usage worldwide by 2023, and has set a 2040 target for net-zero greenhouse gas emissions across its operations.
